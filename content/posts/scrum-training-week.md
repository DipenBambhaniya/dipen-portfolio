Truly-Free arranged training with [Scrum Alliance](https://www.scrumalliance.org/)
for every team, not engineering alone — product, ops, and support sat through
it as well. It ran as roughly a week of sessions, two hours a day, rather than
the usual one-day offsite.

## Why two hours a day, not one day

The backend at Truly-Free is a dozen-odd services — payments, social,
affiliate, livestream, inventory — owned by people who mostly talk to each
other through tickets and standups, not in the same room. A single all-day
workshop is easy to nod through and forget by the next sprint. Two hours a
day for a week means the material has to survive contact with an actual
sprint in progress — you leave a session, go back to your board, and the next
day's session either matches what you just did or it does not.

That mismatch turned out to be the useful part. It surfaced exactly where our
process had drifted from anything resembling shared vocabulary between teams
that ship independently but plan against the same roadmap.

## What it was actually for

Less "learn Scrum from zero" — most of the room had been doing some version
of agile for years — and more forcing a reset on definitions that had quietly
diverged: what counts as done, what belongs in a sprint versus a backlog item,
who owns a blocked ticket that spans two services. Small things, but the kind
that cost a stand-up's worth of clarification every time they come up
undefined.

## What I took from it

Running training across the whole org, not just engineering, is the part
worth repeating. Most of the friction I see day to day is not inside a
service, it's at the handoff between one team's definition of ready and
another's definition of done — and that's not a problem engineering can fix
by itself in a retro.
